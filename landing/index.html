02 — The problem

The speaker in traffic is the problem.

Everyone else is trying to make the speaker hear better. On screen, a noisy audio waveform flattens into a straight line, striking through the words “the speaker”. The heading “Spling deletes the speaker.” appears, and the flat line becomes the dashed rule of a small printed order ticket reading one large latte, oat milk — sound becoming structured data. A human-run drive-through gets the order right about 85 times in 100. The AI voice systems replacing them currently do a little worse. The reasons aren’t mysterious: road noise, cheap speakers, accents, second languages, speech differences. And if the spoken channel fails you entirely — because you’re deaf, because you use an AAC device, because today your voice won’t cooperate — the error rate isn’t 15%. It’s exclusion from a transaction everyone else finishes in ninety seconds.

Remove the acoustic channel, and every failure mode attached to it goes too. You order. They understand. Every time.

03 — What travels with you

What travels with you

  • 01

    Your language

    Portable

    Order in whatever language you think in. The restaurant’s register receives exact items from its own menu, in its own language — never a guess, never free text.

  • 02

    Your profile

    Said once

    How you communicate, what you’re allergic to, what “the usual” means — said once, applied everywhere. An anaphylaxis allergen doesn’t rely on you remembering to mention it; Spling blocks the item before the order exists.

  • 03

    Your record

    Remembered

    Spling keeps both halves of every order: what you asked for, and what actually arrived. Over time that becomes an accuracy record per restaurant — the receipt finally has a memory.

05 — For someone you love

Ordering for someone you love

Sometimes the person ordering isn’t the person eating. A parent, a support worker, a friend can stage the whole order — right items, right modifiers, allergens already enforced — and the person it belongs to finishes it with a single tap. Their order, their confirmation, their independence. Just less typing.
