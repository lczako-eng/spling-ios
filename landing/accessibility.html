Accessibility at Spling

Spling exists because a common transaction — ordering food — quietly excludes people the spoken channel fails: people with speech differences, deaf and hard-of-hearing people, AAC users, and people ordering in a language that isn’t the local one. Accessibility is not a feature of this product. It is the product. So this page is not a legal formality; it is the standard we ask you to hold us to.

What we build to

  • WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor, with AAA contrast for body text.
  • Full keyboard navigation with visible focus indicators on every interactive element.
  • Semantic landmarks and headings, so screen readers can move through the page structurally.
  • The animated conversation on our home page has a complete text narration for screen readers, and respects prefers-reduced-motion — if your system asks for reduced motion, you get a calm, static page.
  • Body text is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, designed by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers.
  • No information is conveyed by color alone; no content flashes.

The product itself

The same standard applies past the website. Spling is used through the assistant you already use — Claude or ChatGPT — in whatever way you already use it: typed, spoken to your own device, or through your AAC setup. Orders are transmitted as structured data, so nothing about your speech, hearing, or language ever needs to pass through a drive-through speaker. Your communication profile is yours: it shapes how ordering works, and it is never shared with merchants.

Where we fall short

If any part of this site or product doesn’t work with your screen reader, switch device, magnifier, AAC system, or anything else you use — that’s a bug, and we’d genuinely like to hear about it. Write to hello@spling.org with whatever detail is easy to share. A person reads these, and we’ll reply.
